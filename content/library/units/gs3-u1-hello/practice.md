# Other ways to run it — Hello

Reach for these when the plan in the map is not working, or when the room needs
something different. None of these introduce new language.

## When the class is too shy to speak

**Halves before pairs.** One side of the room is A, the other is B. A says the
greeting together, B answers together. Nobody is alone. Then split into smaller
groups, then pairs.

**Puppet the exchange.** Put both characters up and do both voices yourself,
slowly, twice. Children who will not speak will often mouth along.

## When they have got it and are bored

**Change the partner, not the task.** The same exchange with a new face is a new
task at this level.

**Speed the rhythm.** Use the chant and clap on each name.

**Take the words away.** Leave only pictures on the board — a wave, two people,
a thumbs-up, a wave goodbye — and let them run the exchange from memory. Put the
words back for any pair that needs them; that is support, not failure.

## When the room is loud or the microphone is poor

Move to choosing rather than saying: play a line and have children hold up one
finger for the first picture, two for the second. You lose productive evidence
but you keep the lesson, and you can still see who is with you.

## When a child has no partner

They work with you. Do the exchange with them at the front only if they want to;
otherwise stand beside them and be their partner quietly.

## Whole-class closes that always work

**The song** (`asset://gs3/audio/track-08.mp3`) — first listen and wave whenever
they hear Hello or Hi, then sing along. Swap in two or three volunteers' names
for the characters' if the class is enjoying it.

**The chant** (`asset://gs3/audio/track-14.mp3`) — listen and clap on each name,
then chant along, then let the class supply the next line when you stop.

Both end with everybody succeeding at the same moment, which is the right feeling
to leave the room on.

## A real reason to use it

A visitor comes to the school, or to the village, or to a family homestay. You
meet them, you ask how they are, you say goodbye when they go. Same language, no
new words, and a reason that exists outside the classroom.

## When the room has one child, and the task asks for pairs

Most of a period is the task cycle, and the task cycle in this unit is written
for children turning to each other. With one child in front of you, those
twenty-odd minutes still have to happen — they are where the language actually
gets used. Do not skip to the next function instead; that is how an hour becomes
six exchanges.

**You are the partner.** Take role B yourself. Do the exchange, then swap: they
open, you answer. Then swap back. That is two turns of practice, not one.

**Be someone else.** Put a character card up and speak as that character —
"Ben says: Hello. I'm Ben." The child answers Ben, not you. Change the card and
they meet someone new, which at this level is a genuinely new task.

**Three partners, three cards.** The map wants three different partners. Show a
different character each round and the target survives with one child in the
room: same exchange, new face, three times.

**Let them be the teacher.** They greet the character; you answer wrongly on
purpose, gently, and let them notice. Children who will not perform will often
correct.

**Use the recording as the partner.** Play the track, pause, and let them answer
the voice. A voice that is not yours is worth a great deal in a room with no
other English speaker.
