# Scaffold down

Repeating the same sentence louder is not teaching. When meaning has not landed,
go down **one** rung, then come straight back up.

## There is no single ladder — start from what failed

Different failures need different moves. Using one universal sequence for all of
them wastes the child's attention on the wrong repair.

### They did not understand the meaning

```
say it once more, unhurried
      ↓
show the picture · gesture · act it out
      ↓
simpler target language, one idea, shorter
      ↓
a concrete example they can see in this room
      ↓
a brief gloss in the school language, if still blocked
```

The picture comes **before** simpler words. At this level a picture carries
meaning faster than replacing incomprehensible language with different
incomprehensible language.

### They understood, but cannot find the word

```
wait — really wait, four seconds
      ↓
a meaning cue: the picture, the category, the situation
      ↓
the first sound, if that is fair for this word
      ↓
model it, and have them say it after you
      ↓
come back to it later, and let them retrieve it alone
```

The last line is the point. An answer produced right after you modelled it is
imitation, not retrieval — see `judge-a-response`.

### They said it, but the form was wrong

```
say the correct version back once, naturally, as a reply
      ↓
leave a beat for them to fix it themselves
      ↓
an explicit cue only if this form is what the period is for
      ↓
keep the conversation going
```

Do not stop a communicative task to repair a form. During free or roleplay
practice, correct less, not more.

### They did not understand the instruction

Not a language failure. Demonstrate the task itself — do it once with the whole
class, or with the adult, before asking anyone to try.

## Rules that hold for all of them

- **One rung at a time.** Jumping to translation teaches them to wait for it.
- **Always climb back.** Every descent ends by returning to the target language
  in the same moment, as an invitation.
- **Never start at the bottom.** The school language is not the medium of
  instruction, and it is not the child's first language either.

## Count the attempts before you choose a rung

`THIS_PERIOD` tells you how many times this objective has been tried and how it
went — `answer-wellbeing x9 (wrong 8, near 1)` is nine attempts, not one hard
moment. `USED_SO_FAR` tells you what you have actually spent: which recordings
you have played, which pictures have been up, whether any exercise has been put
up at all.

Read them together, because they answer a question no single turn can:
**have I been going down a rung, or saying the same rung again?**

If that count is climbing and `USED_SO_FAR` has not changed, the descent is not
happening. Two more of the same is not a third attempt at teaching, it is the
first attempt repeated. Change the *material*, not the wording:

- a recording you have **not** played — `ASSETS` lists what this unit has
- a picture that is **not** the one already up
- an exercise, so they can choose instead of produce
- a different objective from `OBJECTIVES` and come back to this one later

The map's own pacing law is *"time is not the measure; attempts are"*, and it
also says **two new items per ten minutes at most**. Both halves matter: do not
race, and do not idle on one item because it is the one you started with.

## When to leave it for now

A child who has missed the same thing many times in one period is not going to
get it on the next repetition, and the period has other objectives in it.
Record what you saw honestly — `near` and `uncertain` are real answers — put it
down, teach something else, and come back. Carrying the weakest objective into
the next period's opening is what the map asks for; grinding it flat in this one
is not.

## If about a third of the class looks lost

Stop the activity. Do not push through. Go back to the easier version of the
task from earlier in this unit, run it once together, then try again. Note it —
the pacing was wrong, and that is a fact about the material, not about the
children.
