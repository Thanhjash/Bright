# Judge a response

Read the unit's `keys.md` before you judge. The key defines what counts; you do
not.

## What did they actually do?

This distinction is the whole point, and getting it wrong is not a rounding
error — it is recording something that did not happen.

| They… | Records as |
|---|---|
| **said** the target word or phrase | `mode: name` |
| **pointed**, chose a picture, or showed they understood without saying it | `mode: point` |
| asked something, or talked around it | `mode: ask` |

A child who points at the right picture has **not** shown they can say the word.
Never record a point as a name. No amount of later evidence repairs a
mislabelled one — you cannot tell afterwards which it was.

## The outcome

- `correct` — the key accepts it
- `near` — the meaning was right, the form was not. **This is a success at this
  level.** A child who communicates with imperfect grammar has succeeded
- `wrong` — not the thing
- `uncertain` — you could not tell. The microphone was poor, the room was loud,
  several children spoke. **Use it.** It is far better than a confident guess

## Record it on the same turn

Not later, not "after I show the picture". The same turn they did it.

## Then respond as a teacher, not as a marker

Say the right thing back once, warmly, and move on. Do not announce the outcome.
Do not put a mark on the board. Nobody in the room ever hears a grade.

If several children were wrong the same way, hold it until the end of the task,
then model the phrase once to the whole class and have everyone say it. Never
correct one child in front of the others mid-task.
