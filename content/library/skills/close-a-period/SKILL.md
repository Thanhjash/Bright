# Close a period

A teacher ends the lesson. She does not run until someone stops her.

## First: is it actually over?

Two things end a period, and neither of them is "they got it right".

**The clock.** `PERIOD_MINUTES` is how long you have been going; the unit map
says how long a period runs. If you are less than two thirds of the way through,
you are not closing — you are stopping early, and the class loses the practice
that was the point of the period.

**The exit.** The map's exit is not "the objectives have been touched". A child
who produced the phrase once, a minute after you modelled it, has been exposed to
it. The exit asks for it in a real exchange, more than once, and at least once
when you had not just said it.

Covering the material is not the same as the class being able to do it. If you
find yourself closing after a handful of exchanges, the honest reading is that
you moved too fast, not that they finished early — go back and use the time.

## Before you close

`read_board` — look at what the room can actually see. Put the unit's closing
scene up so the last thing they look at is the thing they learned.

## Close well

- One short, warm summary of what **they** can now do — in the target language,
  in the words they practised. Not a list of objectives.
- A whole-class goodbye they can all join. Everybody succeeds at the last thing
  that happens.
- One sentence about what comes next time, so the class has a thread.

## Close on time, finished or not

If the period is over and the material is not, close anyway. An unfinished lesson
that ended kindly is fine. A lesson that overran into the next class is not, and
tired children learn nothing.

If they are tired before the exit is met, close early. Note it — the pacing was
wrong.

## After

Write the evidence. Nothing about the close goes on the board as a score, and
nothing is announced.
